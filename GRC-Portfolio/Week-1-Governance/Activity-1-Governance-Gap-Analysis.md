1. Introduction
This document presents a governance gap analysis of VaultEdge Financial Technologies Ltd., a fictional fintech company operating across multiple countries.
The purpose of the analysis is to identify weaknesses in the company's existing governance arrangements, explain why the gaps matter, and recommend actions to improve accountability, risk management, regulatory compliance, and security oversight.
VaultEdge faces several challenges, including security incidents, missing policies, unclear security responsibilities, unassessed vendors, and customer demands for ISO 27001 certification.
The company's board has approved a budget of $500,000 for its initial security and GRC programme, approved the recruitment of a Chief Information Security Officer (CISO), requested quarterly security reports, and set a target of achieving ISO 27001 certification within 12 months.
2. Priority Classification
The following classifications are used in this analysis:
•	High: A significant governance weakness requiring prompt attention because of its potential impact on security, compliance, business operations, or customer trust.
•	Medium: An important weakness that should be addressed as part of the governance improvement programme, with timing coordinated against more urgent priorities.
These priorities are preliminary recommendations based on the information provided in the training case study. They are not the result of a completed formal risk assessment.
3. Governance Gap Analysis
Gap 1: Governance Charter
Current state: The case study does not identify an approved formal governance charter.
Identified gap: Governance objectives, authority, decision-making responsibilities, and reporting arrangements have not been formally documented.
Recommendation: Develop and obtain approval for a governance charter defining the programme's purpose, scope, responsibilities, decision-making authority, reporting structure, and review process.
Priority: HIGH
Reason: A formal charter will establish the foundation for consistent governance and clarify who is accountable for important decisions.
Gap 2: Dedicated Security Leader / CISO
Current state: The CISO position has been approved but remains vacant. The CTO currently handles security responsibilities informally.
Identified gap: There is no appointed CISO providing dedicated security leadership.
Recommendation: Recruit or appoint a qualified CISO who reports directly to the CEO and has a standing invitation to board meetings, in line with the board's decision.
Priority: HIGH
Reason: Clear executive security leadership is essential for coordinating incident response, managing security risks, overseeing compliance, and reporting progress to senior management.
Gap 3: GRC Function or Team
Current state: VaultEdge has no formal GRC function or GRC manager. One compliance analyst in Berlin focuses on payment regulations.
Identified gap: Governance, risk management, and compliance responsibilities are not organised into a dedicated function with sufficient defined ownership.
Recommendation: Establish a proportionate GRC operating model. Assign interim responsibility for risk management, regulatory obligations, policy management, compliance tracking, and audit preparation while the company develops its longer-term team structure.
Priority: HIGH
Reason: VaultEdge operates across multiple jurisdictions and faces several regulatory and customer assurance requirements. Informal arrangements may not be sufficient to coordinate these obligations.
Gap 4: IT and Security Governance Policies
Current state: The case study identifies missing formal policies, including acceptable use, password, data classification, and access control policies.
Identified gap: Employees and technical teams lack a complete set of documented and approved rules for handling information and managing technology securely.
Recommendation: Develop, approve, communicate, and periodically review security policies. Initial priorities should include access control, information classification, acceptable use, secrets management, incident response, and change management.
Priority: HIGH
Reason: Documented policies help employees understand their responsibilities and give management a consistent basis for enforcing security requirements.
Gap 5: Risk Appetite Statement
Current state: The case study does not identify a formally approved risk appetite statement.
Identified gap: The company's tolerance for different categories of risk and its approach to accepting or escalating risks have not been formally documented.
Recommendation: Develop a risk appetite statement for board approval. It should address areas such as customer-data protection, regulatory compliance, service availability, third-party risk, and the authority to accept residual risks.
Priority: HIGH
Reason: A risk appetite statement helps management make consistent decisions about which risks require immediate treatment, escalation, or formal acceptance.
Gap 6: IT and Security Steering Committee
Current state: The case study does not identify a formally established IT or security steering committee.
Identified gap: A structured forum for coordinating security priorities, business needs, risk decisions, and governance activities has not been documented.
Recommendation: Establish a cross-functional steering committee with appropriate representation from executive management, technology, product, operations, finance, and GRC. Define its responsibilities, membership, meeting frequency, and decision-making authority.
Priority: MEDIUM
Reason: A steering committee can improve coordination and help ensure that security and governance decisions consider both business objectives and risk exposure.
Gap 7: Governance Framework Adoption
Current state: The case study does not state that VaultEdge has formally adopted a governance framework. The board has established a target of ISO 27001 certification within 12 months.
Identified gap: A formally approved framework and documented implementation approach have not been identified.
Recommendation: Select and approve a suitable governance and information security framework approach, aligned with the ISO 27001 target, relevant regulatory obligations, and customer requirements.
Priority: HIGH
Reason: An agreed framework provides structure for defining controls, assigning responsibilities, measuring implementation progress, and preparing evidence for certification and customer assurance.
Gap 8: Board Reporting on Security and IT
Current state: The board has requested quarterly security reports immediately. However, the case study does not describe an established reporting process, standard report format, or agreed set of measures.
Identified gap: The board's reporting requirement has been established, but the process for consistently delivering decision-useful reports is not documented.
Recommendation: Create a quarterly board-reporting process covering significant risks, security incidents, compliance obligations, control weaknesses, remediation progress, ISO 27001 readiness, and decisions requiring board attention.
Priority: HIGH
Reason: Regular reporting will help the board understand the company's security position, monitor progress, and provide appropriate oversight.
Gap 9: Three Lines Model
Current state: The case study does not describe a formally defined Three Lines Model or equivalent arrangement for separating risk ownership, oversight, and independent assurance.
Identified gap: The distinction between operational responsibility for managing risks, oversight of risk and compliance, and independent assurance has not been documented.
Recommendation: Define responsibilities proportionately to the company's size and resources:
•	First line: Business and operational teams own and manage the risks arising from their activities.
•	Second line: Risk, compliance, and security oversight functions establish guidance, monitor exposure, and challenge risk management where necessary.
•	Third line: Independent assurance evaluates whether governance and risk-management arrangements are designed and operating effectively.
Priority: MEDIUM
Reason: Clearly defined responsibilities can reduce confusion, strengthen accountability, and improve confidence in the effectiveness of risk management.
Gap 10: Governance Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)
Current state: The case study does not identify formally defined governance KPIs.
Identified gap: There is no documented set of measures for tracking governance implementation and reporting progress to leadership.
Recommendation: Define measurable indicators with assigned owners, data sources, reporting frequency, and targets. Possible indicators include:
•	Percentage of overdue high-risk remediation actions.
•	Percentage of required access reviews completed on time.
•	Percentage of priority security policies approved and communicated.
•	Time taken to detect, escalate, and respond to security incidents.
•	Number of overdue vendor risk assessments.
•	Progress against ISO 27001 readiness milestones.
Priority: HIGH
Reason: KPIs help management and the board monitor implementation, identify delays, and determine whether governance improvements are progressing as planned.
4. Key Findings
The analysis identifies significant gaps in VaultEdge's governance arrangements.
The most urgent findings concern the absence of dedicated security leadership, the lack of a formal GRC function, missing security policies, the absence of a documented risk appetite, and the need for an organised framework and reporting process.
These weaknesses are particularly important because the case study describes:
•	AWS access credentials exposed through a public GitHub repository.
•	A phishing incident involving potential access to approximately 15,000 EU customer records.
•	Former employees retaining active access to company systems.
•	Vendors that have not been assessed.
•	Incomplete security monitoring and the absence of centralised SIEM monitoring.
•	Customer requirements for ISO 27001 certification or equivalent assurance.
These facts indicate that governance improvements should be coordinated with practical security and risk-reduction activities.
5. Recommended Initial Actions
The following actions are recommended as starting points for the governance programme:
1.	Confirm executive sponsorship and interim ownership of security and GRC responsibilities while the CISO position is vacant.
2.	Develop and approve the governance charter.
3.	Establish a central register of governance gaps, risks, actions, owners, deadlines, and progress.
4.	Prioritise access management, incident response, secrets management, and change management improvements.
5.	Develop the initial policy set and assign policy owners.
6.	Establish a documented process for quarterly board security reporting.
7.	Select an appropriate framework approach and create an ISO 27001 implementation roadmap.
8.	Define governance KPIs and review progress regularly.
These actions should be coordinated within the approved budget and the company's 12-month certification target.
6. Conclusion
The VaultEdge case study indicates that the company needs a more formal and coordinated governance programme to support its growth, security responsibilities, regulatory obligations, and customer commitments.
Establishing clear accountability, documented policies, structured risk management, regular board reporting, and measurable governance objectives will provide a foundation for improving oversight and supporting the ISO 27001 certification target.
This gap analysis provides a starting point for the remaining Week 1 activities, including the governance charter, organisational structure, RACI matrix, risk appetite statement, framework selection, reporting structure, and governance KPIs.
7. Learning Reflection
Through this exercise, I learned that GRC is not limited to writing policies or checking regulatory requirements. It also involves establishing accountability, helping leadership make informed risk decisions, monitoring implementation, and providing evidence that governance arrangements are working.
I also learned that a governance gap analysis should connect each finding to evidence, explain its business significance, assign a justified priority, and recommend a practical action.
