1. Introduction
A Risk Appetite Statement defines the level and type of risk an organisation is willing to accept while achieving its business objectives.
It provides guidance to management and employees when making decisions that may expose the organisation to financial, operational, technological, legal, regulatory or cybersecurity risks.
VaultEdge Financial Technologies is a growing fintech organisation that processes millions of financial transactions and handles sensitive customer information across multiple countries.
Due to its business operations, regulatory obligations and customer expectations, VaultEdge must maintain a low tolerance for risks that could compromise customer information, payment services, regulatory compliance or business continuity.
This document proposes a risk appetite framework to guide the organisation's risk management decisions.
2. Purpose of the Risk Appetite Statement
The purpose of this statement is to:
1.	Establish clear boundaries for the risks VaultEdge is willing to accept.
2.	Protect customer information and financial transactions.
3.	Support compliance with relevant regulatory and contractual requirements.
4.	Guide management when making business and technology decisions.
5.	Help prioritise cybersecurity and operational risks.
6.	Support the organisation's ISO 27001 certification objective.
7.	Strengthen accountability for identifying, managing and reporting risks.
3. Risk Appetite Categories
VaultEdge's proposed risk appetite is classified into four levels:
Risk Appetite Level	Meaning
Zero / No Appetite	The organisation will not knowingly accept this type of risk.
Low	Only limited risk is acceptable, and strong controls must be maintained.
Moderate	Some risk may be accepted when justified by business benefits and appropriate controls.
High	The organisation is willing to accept significant risk to pursue an opportunity.
Important: Zero appetite does not mean that risk can be completely eliminated. It means the organisation does not knowingly accept the specified exposure and must take reasonable steps to prevent or reduce it.
4. Proposed Risk Appetite by Category
Risk Category	Proposed Appetite	Explanation
Customer Data Protection	Zero / No Appetite	VaultEdge must not knowingly tolerate unauthorised access, disclosure or misuse of sensitive customer information.
Regulatory Compliance	Zero / No Appetite	The organisation has a very low tolerance for deliberate or avoidable regulatory violations.
Payment Processing Security	Low	Payment systems must be protected against fraud, unauthorised access and service disruption.
Cybersecurity Threats	Low	Risks such as phishing, ransomware, credential theft and data breaches must be actively managed.
Business Continuity	Low	Critical services should remain available, with suitable recovery arrangements.
Third-Party / Vendor Risk	Low	Vendors handling customer data or accessing critical systems must undergo appropriate security assessment.
Technology Innovation	Moderate	New technologies may be adopted when security risks are assessed and appropriate controls are implemented.
Business Expansion	Moderate	Expansion into new markets may be accepted after regulatory, operational and security risks are evaluated.
Financial Risk	Low	Financial exposures must be managed within approved financial controls and risk limits.
Employee Security Behaviour	Low	Employees must follow security policies and report suspicious activities promptly.
5. Risk Appetite Rationale
The proposed appetite levels are based on VaultEdge's business model, current security weaknesses and regulatory exposure.
Customer Data Protection – Zero / No Appetite
VaultEdge Wallet stores personal information such as names, dates of birth, addresses, government identification and bank details.
The organisation must therefore maintain strong controls to prevent unauthorised access, disclosure or misuse of customer information.
Regulatory Compliance – Zero / No Appetite
VaultEdge operates across the UK, European Union, Kenya and Singapore.
Its regulatory obligations include GDPR, the UK regulatory environment, Kenya's Data Protection Act, Singapore's PDPA and PCI DSS requirements relevant to its card-processing activities.
Failure to meet applicable obligations could lead to regulatory penalties, loss of customer confidence and contractual consequences.
Cybersecurity – Low Appetite
VaultEdge has experienced two significant security incidents:
•	Exposure of AWS access keys through a public GitHub repository.
•	A phishing incident that resulted in unauthorised access to a customer support tool containing information relating to approximately 15,000 EU customers.
These incidents demonstrate the need for stronger access management, employee awareness, monitoring and incident response controls.
Third-Party Risk – Low Appetite
VaultEdge works with 23 vendors that have not undergone formal security assessments.
The organisation should assess vendors according to their access to systems, sensitive information and business-critical services.
Technology Innovation – Moderate Appetite
As a growing fintech company, VaultEdge needs to innovate and improve its products.
However, new technologies must be evaluated for security, privacy, compliance and operational risks before deployment.
6. Risk Appetite Governance and Responsibilities
Role	Responsibility
Board of Directors	Approves the organisation's risk appetite and oversees significant risk exposure.
CEO	Ensures that business decisions remain within the approved risk appetite.
CISO	Identifies and oversees information security risks and recommends appropriate controls.
GRC Lead	Coordinates risk assessments, documents risk appetite requirements and monitors compliance.
CTO	Implements technical safeguards and manages technology-related risks.
Business and Regional Leaders	Identify and manage risks within their departments and regional operations.
7. Risk Escalation and Reporting
Risks that exceed the approved appetite must be documented and escalated to the appropriate management level.
The following process is recommended:
1.	Identify the risk.
2.	Assess its likelihood and potential impact.
3.	Compare the assessed risk with the approved appetite.
4.	Identify and implement appropriate risk treatment measures.
5.	Escalate risks that exceed approved tolerance to the CISO, GRC Lead and relevant executive management.
6.	Escalate material risks to the CEO and Board where necessary.
7.	Monitor the risk until it is reduced, accepted through proper authority, transferred or avoided.
No employee should independently accept a significant risk outside their delegated authority.
8. Review and Approval
This Risk Appetite Statement should be reviewed at least annually and whenever significant changes occur in VaultEdge's business operations, technology environment, regulatory obligations or threat landscape.
The Board of Directors is responsible for approving the final statement.
Proposed Approval:
•	Prepared by: GRC Project Team
•	Reviewed by: Proposed CISO and GRC Lead
•	Executive Sponsor: CEO
•	Final Approval: Board of Directors
•	Review Frequency: Annually, or following significant changes
9. Conclusion
The proposed Risk Appetite Statement provides VaultEdge with a clear direction for managing risks while pursuing its business objectives.
By maintaining a very low tolerance for customer data exposure, regulatory non-compliance and critical cybersecurity failures, VaultEdge can strengthen customer trust and protect its financial and operational interests.
A clearly defined risk appetite will also help management prioritise security investments, support ISO 27001 implementation and improve the organisation's overall governance programme.
