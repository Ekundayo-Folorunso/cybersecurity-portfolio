1. Introduction
An effective governance programme requires regular meetings, clear communication channels and structured reporting.
Meeting cadence refers to how frequently governance meetings take place, while reporting structure defines how important information is communicated to the appropriate management levels.
VaultEdge Financial Technologies currently has weaknesses in cybersecurity governance, including informal security management, limited risk reporting, a vacant CISO position and no established GRC function.
The Board has already directed that quarterly security reports should be introduced immediately.
This document proposes a meeting and reporting structure to improve accountability, risk visibility, decision-making and communication across the organisation.
2. Objectives
The proposed meeting cadence and reporting structure will:
1.	Establish regular oversight of cybersecurity and technology risks.
2.	Ensure management receives timely information about security incidents.
3.	Improve communication between the Board, executive management, CISO, GRC and business leaders.
4.	Monitor progress towards ISO 27001 certification.
5.	Track regulatory and compliance obligations.
6.	Review outstanding risks and corrective actions.
7.	Support informed decision-making and accountability.
3. Proposed Governance Meetings
The following meetings are recommended for VaultEdge.
3.1 Board Security and Risk Oversight Meeting
Frequency: Quarterly
Chair: Board Chair or designated Board Committee Chair
Participants:
•	Board of Directors
•	CEO – Sarah Chen
•	CISO – Approved but currently vacant position
•	GRC Lead – Proposed role
•	CTO – David Okonkwo, when required
Purpose:
To provide strategic oversight of cybersecurity, enterprise risk and regulatory compliance.
Key Agenda Items:
•	Overall cybersecurity risk exposure.
•	Significant security incidents and lessons learned.
•	Progress towards ISO 27001 certification.
•	Regulatory and contractual compliance.
•	Major technology and business risks.
•	Security budget and resource requirements.
•	Outstanding high-risk issues.
•	Governance performance and KPI results.
Expected Outputs:
•	Board decisions and approvals.
•	Security improvement priorities.
•	Risk acceptance or treatment decisions within Board authority.
•	Recorded meeting minutes and action owners.
3.2 Executive Risk and Governance Committee Meeting
Frequency: Monthly
Chair: CEO – Sarah Chen
Participants:
•	CEO
•	CISO
•	GRC Lead
•	CTO
•	CFO – Maria Santos
•	VP Engineering – James Mitchell

Purpose:
To review the organisation's overall risk position and ensure that security and compliance activities support business objectives.
Key Agenda Items:

•	Major cybersecurity and operational risks.
•	Security budget and resource allocation.
•	Regulatory compliance progress.
•	Vendor and third-party risks.

3.3 Security Operations and Technical Review Meeting
Frequency: Weekly
Chair: CISO
Participants:
•	CISO
•	CTO
•	VP Engineering
•	IT and security personnel
•	Relevant system and application owners
Purpose:
To review technical security activities and identify weaknesses requiring attention.
Key Agenda Items:

•	Vulnerability assessment and remediation.
•	System access and privileged accounts.
•	Security monitoring and logging.
•	Patch management.
•	Cloud security configuration.

Expected Outputs:
•	Technical remediation actions.
•	Updated vulnerability records.

•	Assigned technical responsibilities.
3.4 GRC and Compliance Review Meeting
Frequency: Bi-weekly
Chair: GRC Lead
Participants:
•	GRC Lead
•	CISO
•	CTO representatives
•	Compliance and legal representatives
•	Relevant business and regional leaders
Purpose:
To coordinate governance, risk management and compliance activities.
Key Agenda Items:
•	Policy development and review.
•	Risk assessment updates.
•	Regulatory compliance obligations.
•	Vendor risk assessments.
•	ISO 27001 documentation and implementation.
•	Control testing and evidence collection.
•	Audit findings and corrective actions.
•	Compliance deadlines.
Expected Outputs:
•	Updated compliance tracker.
•	Updated risk and issue registers.
•	Policy review actions.
•	Control improvement recommendations.
3.5 Incident Response Meeting
Frequency: As required when a significant security incident occurs.
Chair: CISO or designated incident response leader
Participants:
•	CISO
•	CTO
•	GRC Lead
•	Legal and privacy representatives
•	Relevant business leaders
•	Communications representatives when required
Purpose:
To coordinate the organisation's response to cybersecurity incidents.
Key Agenda Items:
•	Nature and scope of the incident.
•	Systems and information affected.
•	Immediate containment measures.
•	Investigation findings.
•	Legal and regulatory notification requirements.
•	Customer and stakeholder communication.
•	Recovery actions.
•	Lessons learned and corrective actions.
Expected Outputs:
•	Incident response decisions.
•	Documented actions and responsibilities.
•	Escalation to executive management where necessary.
•	Incident report and post-incident review.
Important: Serious incidents must be escalated promptly through the incident reporting process. The organisation must not wait for a scheduled meeting before responding.

7. Quarterly Board Security Report – Proposed Contents
The quarterly Board report should be concise and focused on information that supports strategic decisions.
It should include:
1.	Executive Summary: Overall security and governance position.
2.	Major Security Incidents: Significant incidents, impact and corrective actions.
3.	Risk Exposure: High and critical risks requiring management attention.
4.	Compliance Status: Progress against relevant legal, regulatory and contractual obligations.
5.	ISO 27001 Progress: Implementation milestones and outstanding gaps.
6.	Security Performance: Results of approved governance and security KPIs.
7.	Budget and Resources: Security spending and additional resource requirements.
8.	Management Decisions Required: Issues requiring Board approval or direction.
8. Escalation and Communication Process
VaultEdge should establish an incident and risk escalation process.
The proposed process is:
1.	An employee or monitoring system identifies a security issue.
2.	The issue is reported immediately through the approved incident reporting channel.
3.	The security team or designated incident lead assesses its severity.
4.	Relevant technical and business teams take appropriate containment and investigation actions.
5.	Significant incidents are escalated to the CISO and CEO.
6.	Legal and privacy representatives assess applicable notification obligations.
7.	Material risks and incidents are reported to the Board as appropriate.
8.	The organisation documents the incident, recovery actions and lessons learned.
Regulatory and customer notifications must follow applicable legal, contractual and regulatory timelines.
9. Implementation Recommendations
To establish the proposed meeting and reporting arrangements, VaultEdge should:
1.	Recruit the approved CISO.
2.	Establish the GRC function and appoint a GRC Lead.
3.	Approve the governance meeting calendar.
4.	Define meeting terms of reference and participant responsibilities.
5.	Introduce standard meeting agendas and minutes.
6.	Create templates for risk, compliance and security reporting.
7.	Establish clear incident escalation procedures.
8.	Track meeting actions and deadlines.
9.	Review the effectiveness of the reporting structure annually.
10. Conclusion
The proposed meeting cadence and reporting structure will provide VaultEdge with a consistent approach to cybersecurity governance and risk oversight.
Regular meetings will help management identify issues, review risks, monitor compliance and track corrective actions.
Clear reporting channels will ensure that significant security information reaches the appropriate decision-makers without unnecessary delays.
These arrangements will strengthen accountability, support the organisation's ISO 27001 certification objective and improve the overall effectiveness of its GRC programme.
