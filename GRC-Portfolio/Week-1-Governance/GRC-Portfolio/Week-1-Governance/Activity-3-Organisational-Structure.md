## 1. Introduction

An organisational structure defines how responsibilities, authority, communication, and reporting relationships are arranged within an organisation.

For Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC), an effective organisational structure ensures that security responsibilities are clearly assigned, risks are properly managed, and compliance activities receive appropriate oversight.

VaultEdge Financial Technologies currently has approximately 450 employees operating across different countries. However, the company has no formal GRC function, and its approved CISO position remains vacant. Security responsibilities are currently handled informally by the CTO.

This activity proposes an organisational structure that establishes clear accountability for information security, risk management, compliance, and board reporting.

## 2. Objectives of the Proposed Structure

The proposed organisational structure aims to:

1. Establish clear security leadership and accountability.
2. Ensure the CISO reports directly to the CEO, as directed by the board.
3. Create a formal Governance, Risk, and Compliance function.
4. Separate business operations from risk oversight and independent assurance.
5. Improve coordination between technology, business, security, and compliance teams.
6. Support regulatory compliance and the ISO 27001 certification target.
7. Provide the board with regular information about security risks and governance performance.

## 4. Roles and Responsibilities

### 4.1 Board of Directors

**Position:** Highest governance oversight authority.

Responsibilities:

- Approve the governance charter and major governance policies.
- Provide oversight of significant business and security risks.
- Review quarterly security and GRC reports.
- Monitor progress toward ISO 27001 certification.
- Ensure management provides adequate resources for the governance programme.
- Challenge management on unresolved significant risks.

### 4.2 Chief Executive Officer (CEO) — Sarah Chen

**Position:** Executive leadership and programme sponsor.

Responsibilities:

- Provide executive sponsorship for the governance programme.
- Ensure that governance activities support business objectives.
- Oversee the appointment and effectiveness of the CISO.
- Support the establishment of the GRC function.
- Ensure significant risks are escalated appropriately.
- Report major governance matters to the board.

### 4.3 Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) — Approved, Vacant

**Position:** Direct report to the CEO.

Responsibilities:

- Lead the company's information security programme.
- Develop and coordinate the security strategy.
- Monitor security risks and control weaknesses.
- Coordinate security incident management.
- Oversee security policies, standards, and procedures.
- Work with technology and business teams to improve security.
- Coordinate security reporting to executive management and the board.
- Support the ISO 27001 implementation programme.

**Important:** The CISO position has been approved by the board but has not yet been filled. The company should appoint an interim security lead until recruitment is completed.

### 4.4 Head of Governance, Risk and Compliance (GRC) — Proposed

**Position:** Proposed GRC leadership role.

Responsibilities:

- Coordinate the GRC programme.
- Maintain the enterprise risk register.
- Track regulatory and contractual obligations.
- Coordinate governance policies and procedures.
- Monitor compliance activities.
- Track remediation actions and governance KPIs.
- Coordinate audit preparation and compliance evidence.
- Support ISO 27001 readiness activities.

**Proposed reporting arrangement:** The Head of GRC should have direct access to the CEO and appropriate access to the board for significant risk and compliance matters. The role should work closely with the CISO while retaining the ability to independently challenge and report governance and compliance concerns.

This arrangement is proposed to strengthen oversight and avoid placing all security, risk, and compliance responsibilities within one operational function.

### 4.5 Chief Technology Officer (CTO) — David Okonkwo

**Position:** Technology leadership.

Responsibilities:

- Oversee technology infrastructure and engineering operations.
- Ensure technical security controls are implemented.
- Support vulnerability management and system monitoring.
- Ensure appropriate access controls are applied.
- Coordinate secure development and change management.
- Support incident investigation and remediation.
- Provide technical evidence for security assessments.

The CTO should work closely with the CISO but should not be the sole owner of the company's security governance programme.

### 4.6 Chief Financial Officer (CFO) — Maria Santos

Responsibilities:

- Support budgeting and financial planning for GRC activities.
- Monitor the financial implications of significant risks.
- Support resource allocation for security improvements.
- Provide financial oversight for the approved $500,000 programme budget.

### 4.7 Head of Product — Priya Sharma

Responsibilities:

- Ensure security and privacy considerations are included in product development.
- Coordinate with security teams during product changes.
- Support secure product design and development.
- Ensure product-related risks are identified and escalated.

### 4.8 Regional Operations Leaders

**Hans Weber — Head of European Operations**

**Rachel Tan — Head of APAC Operations**

**Amina Nwosu — Head of Africa Operations**

Responsibilities:

- Ensure regional teams follow approved company security policies.
- Identify and escalate regional operational and compliance risks.
- Support employee security awareness.
- Coordinate with the GRC function on local regulatory obligations.
- Support incident reporting and investigation.
- Ensure appropriate access is removed when employees leave.

### 4.9 Independent Assurance

**Proposed function:** Internal Audit or an appropriately independent external assurance provider.

Responsibilities:

- Independently assess the design and effectiveness of governance and risk controls.
- Evaluate whether management's risk-management processes are operating effectively.
- Report significant findings to the appropriate oversight authority.
- Follow up on agreed audit recommendations.

VaultEdge's case study does not identify an existing internal audit department. This is therefore a proposed assurance arrangement, not an existing company function.

## 5. The Three Lines Model

The proposed organisational structure should support the Three Lines Model.

This model helps organisations distinguish between operational risk ownership, risk oversight, and independent assurance.

### First Line — Business and Operational Management

**Who:** Technology teams, product teams, finance, and regional operations.

**Responsibilities:**

- Own and manage risks arising from daily activities.
- Follow approved policies and procedures.
- Implement assigned controls.
- Report incidents and control weaknesses.
- Correct identified operational problems.

**VaultEdge example:** The technology team manages system access, applies security patches, and protects development credentials.

### Second Line — Risk, Compliance and Security Oversight

**Who:** Head of GRC and relevant security oversight personnel, including the CISO.

**Responsibilities:**

- Develop risk-management guidance.
- Monitor compliance obligations.
- Provide oversight and challenge to operational teams.
- Track significant risks and remediation actions.
- Report governance and security performance.

**VaultEdge example:** The GRC function monitors vendor risk assessments, regulatory obligations, and overdue risk treatment actions.

### Third Line — Independent Assurance

**Who:** Internal Audit or an appropriately independent external assurance provider.

**Responsibilities:**

- Independently evaluate governance and risk-management arrangements.
- Assess whether controls are working as intended.
- Identify weaknesses and recommend improvements.
- Provide independent assurance to the appropriate oversight authority.

**VaultEdge example:** An independent audit assesses whether access reviews are being performed and whether the evidence supports the company's control requirements.

**Important distinction:** The CISO leads the security programme and provides security oversight. The GRC function coordinates risk and compliance activities. Independent assurance should be sufficiently separate from the activities it evaluates.

## 7. Implementation Recommendations

To implement the proposed structure, VaultEdge should:

1. Recruit a qualified CISO to fill the approved position.
2. Assign an interim security lead while recruitment is ongoing.
3. Establish a formal GRC function and appoint a GRC lead.
4. Approve clear role descriptions and reporting relationships.
5. Define the responsibilities of the first, second, and third lines.
6. Establish communication and escalation procedures between business, technology, security, and GRC teams.
7. Define an independent assurance arrangement.
8. Ensure that regional operations understand their governance responsibilities.
9. Align the structure with the governance charter and RACI matrix.
10. Review the structure periodically as the company grows.

## 8. Expected Benefits

The proposed structure should provide the following benefits:

- Clearer accountability for security and compliance.
- Stronger executive oversight of technology and business risks.
- Improved coordination between GRC and operational teams.
- Better regulatory and contractual compliance management.
- More effective incident escalation and reporting.
- Improved preparation for ISO 27001 certification.
- Stronger confidence among customers, investors, and other stakeholders.

## 9. Conclusion

VaultEdge requires a formal organisational structure to support its governance programme and address existing weaknesses in security leadership, risk management, and compliance oversight.

The proposed structure establishes direct CISO reporting to the CEO, creates a formal GRC leadership function, clarifies the responsibilities of business and technology teams, and introduces independent assurance.

Once approved, this structure should guide the development of the RACI matrix, governance reporting arrangements, and risk management procedures.
