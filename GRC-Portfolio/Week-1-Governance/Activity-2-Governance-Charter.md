1. Purpose
The purpose of this Governance Charter is to establish a formal structure for governing information security, risk management, and compliance at VaultEdge Financial Technologies Ltd.
The charter defines the objectives, responsibilities, authority, and reporting arrangements required to strengthen governance, protect company and customer information, meet applicable regulatory obligations, and support business growth.
VaultEdge has experienced security incidents and faces increasing expectations from customers, regulators, and investors. A formal governance programme is therefore needed to improve accountability and provide effective oversight of security and technology risks.
2. Scope
This charter applies to VaultEdge's business operations, employees, management, relevant contractors, information systems, and third parties that support its services or process information on its behalf.
The governance programme covers:
•	Information security governance and oversight.
•	Risk identification, assessment, treatment, and reporting.
•	Regulatory and contractual compliance.
•	Security policies, standards, and procedures.
•	Access control and identity management.
•	Security incident management and escalation.
•	Third-party and supplier risk management.
•	Business continuity and disaster recovery governance.
•	Security awareness and employee responsibilities.
•	Security monitoring, reporting, and performance measurement.
•	Preparation for ISO 27001 certification.
The programme should consider VaultEdge's operations in the United Kingdom, Germany, Singapore, Kenya, and other locations where it operates or processes information.
3. Governance Objectives
The objectives of the governance programme are to:
1.	Establish clear accountability for information security and GRC activities.
2.	Improve the identification, assessment, treatment, and monitoring of business and technology risks.
3.	Protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of company and customer information.
4.	Support compliance with applicable legal, regulatory, and contractual obligations.
5.	Establish approved security policies and consistent operating procedures.
6.	Improve incident reporting, escalation, investigation, and lessons learned.
7.	Strengthen oversight of suppliers and third-party service providers.
8.	Provide the board with regular, meaningful security and risk reports.
9.	Support the board's target of achieving ISO 27001 certification within 12 months.
10.	Provide evidence of governance progress to customers and investors.
4. Governance Principles
VaultEdge's governance programme should operate according to the following principles:
Accountability: Every significant governance responsibility must have an assigned owner.
Risk-based decision-making: Security and compliance decisions should consider the likelihood and potential impact of risks on the business.
Regulatory awareness: The company should identify and assess the legal, regulatory, and contractual obligations applicable to its operations.
Proportionate controls: Security measures should reflect the sensitivity of information, business requirements, and level of risk.
Transparency and reporting: Significant risks, incidents, control weaknesses, and remediation progress should be reported to the appropriate management level.
Continuous improvement: Governance arrangements should be reviewed regularly and improved when weaknesses, incidents, or changing requirements are identified.
5. Governance Structure and Responsibilities
5.1 Board of Directors
The Board of Directors provides oversight of the governance programme.
Its responsibilities include:
•	Approving the governance charter and key governance policies.
•	Providing oversight of significant security and compliance risks.
•	Reviewing quarterly security reports.
•	Monitoring progress toward ISO 27001 certification.
•	Reviewing significant incidents and unresolved high-risk issues.
•	Ensuring that appropriate resources are allocated to the governance programme.
•	Challenging management where risk treatment or remediation progress is inadequate.
5.2 Chief Executive Officer (CEO)
The CEO provides executive sponsorship for the governance programme.
Responsibilities include:
•	Ensuring that governance activities support business objectives.
•	Supporting the recruitment and authority of the CISO.
•	Resolving significant cross-functional governance issues.
•	Ensuring that executive management responds to significant risks.
•	Escalating matters requiring board decisions.
5.3 Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)
The CISO is responsible for coordinating the information security programme and advising leadership on security risks.
Responsibilities include:
•	Developing and coordinating the information security strategy.
•	Maintaining visibility of significant security risks.
•	Coordinating security policies, standards, and control improvements.
•	Supporting incident management and escalation.
•	Monitoring security programme performance.
•	Coordinating ISO 27001 implementation and readiness activities.
•	Preparing security reports for executive management and the board.
•	Working with relevant teams to improve security awareness and risk management.
The CISO role has been approved by the board but is currently vacant. Until the role is filled, the CEO should assign interim responsibility for these activities and document the temporary arrangements.
5.4 GRC Function
The GRC function is responsible for coordinating governance documentation, risk processes, and compliance tracking.
Its proposed responsibilities include:
•	Maintaining the risk register and governance action register.
•	Tracking applicable regulatory and contractual obligations.
•	Coordinating policy reviews and approvals.
•	Monitoring remediation activities.
•	Supporting internal assessments and audit preparation.
•	Tracking governance KPIs and reporting progress.
•	Coordinating evidence collection for ISO 27001 readiness.
VaultEdge does not currently have a formal GRC function. The responsibilities above are proposed arrangements that should be resourced and assigned by management.
5.5 Chief Technology Officer (CTO) and Technology Teams
The CTO and technology teams are responsible for implementing and operating security controls within the technology environment.
Responsibilities include:
•	Maintaining secure technology configurations.
•	Managing system access and technical vulnerabilities.
•	Supporting security monitoring and incident investigations.
•	Applying approved change-management procedures.
•	Protecting credentials, secrets, and development environments.
•	Supporting asset inventory and access reviews.
•	Providing evidence that relevant technical controls are operating.
5.6 Business and Operational Leaders
Business and operational leaders are responsible for managing risks within their areas of responsibility.
They should:
•	Ensure staff follow approved policies.
•	Identify and escalate risks and control weaknesses.
•	Support access reviews and employee offboarding.
•	Ensure business processes comply with applicable requirements.
•	Cooperate with incident investigations and remediation activities.
5.7 Employees and Contractors
All employees and relevant contractors are expected to:
•	Follow approved security policies.
•	Protect company credentials and customer information.
•	Complete required security awareness activities.
•	Report suspected incidents promptly.
•	Use company systems and data appropriately.
•	Cooperate with access reviews and security investigations.
6. Decision-Making and Escalation
Governance decisions should be made at the appropriate level according to their significance, business impact, and risk.
•	Routine operational risks should be managed by the responsible operational or technology owner.
•	Significant security and compliance issues should be escalated to the CISO or designated interim security lead and relevant executive management.
•	Material risks requiring executive decisions should be escalated to the CEO.
•	Matters requiring board oversight, approval, or acceptance should be presented to the Board of Directors through the agreed reporting and escalation process.
•	Suspected security incidents should be handled under an approved incident response procedure, including any required legal, regulatory, and customer notification assessments.
Risk acceptance authority and escalation thresholds must be documented in a supporting risk management procedure. This charter does not establish specific financial or risk acceptance limits.
7. Governance Meetings and Reporting
The following arrangements are proposed for approval.
Board reporting: Quarterly security and GRC reports, as already requested by the board.
Executive governance review: Regular reviews of significant risks, incidents, compliance obligations, resource requirements, and overdue remediation actions.
Security and GRC coordination: Regular coordination meetings between the CISO or interim security lead, GRC personnel, technology teams, and relevant business representatives.
Incident escalation: Significant incidents should be escalated promptly in accordance with the incident response procedure.
The meeting frequency, membership, report templates, and escalation deadlines should be documented in the governance operating procedures.
8. Risk Management Requirements
VaultEdge should establish a documented risk management process covering:
1.	Identification of risks to business operations, information, systems, and regulatory compliance.
2.	Assessment of risk likelihood and potential impact.
3.	Evaluation of risks against approved risk criteria and risk appetite.
4.	Selection of risk treatment options.
5.	Assignment of risk owners and remediation deadlines.
6.	Monitoring of risk treatment progress.
7.	Escalation and formal approval of risks that require acceptance.
8.	Reporting of significant risks to executive management and the board.
The company should maintain a central risk register and ensure that significant risks have documented owners, treatment plans, and review dates.
9. Compliance and Framework Alignment
The governance programme should identify and assess applicable requirements, including:
•	PCI DSS, because VaultPay processes payment-card data.
•	GDPR, including requirements relevant to the processing of EU personal data.
•	Applicable UK financial-services regulatory requirements.
•	Kenya's Data Protection Act.
•	Singapore's Personal Data Protection Act.
•	Relevant contractual security and assurance requirements from customers.
•	ISO 27001 requirements supporting the board's certification target.
The applicability, scope, and current compliance status of each requirement must be formally assessed. This charter does not establish that VaultEdge is compliant with any of these requirements.
The selected framework and detailed implementation roadmap should be documented in the separate framework selection and compliance planning activities.
10. Performance Monitoring
The governance programme should use measurable indicators to track implementation and effectiveness.
Proposed indicators include:
•	Progress against ISO 27001 readiness milestones.
•	Number of overdue high-risk remediation actions.
•	Completion rate of required access reviews.
•	Status of priority security policy approvals.
•	Completion of third-party risk assessments.
•	Security incident response and remediation performance.
•	Completion of required security awareness activities.
•	Status of significant compliance gaps.
Each KPI should have a defined owner, measurement method, reporting frequency, and target. The final KPI set should be approved through the governance process.
11. Resources and Implementation
The board has approved an initial budget of $500,000 for the security and GRC programme.
Management should allocate this budget according to documented priorities, business needs, and assessed risks.
Initial implementation priorities should include:
1.	Filling the approved CISO position and assigning interim security responsibilities.
2.	Establishing the governance charter and accountability structure.
3.	Addressing urgent access management and credential-handling weaknesses.
4.	Improving incident response and escalation arrangements.
5.	Developing essential security policies and risk management procedures.
6.	Establishing the risk register and governance reporting process.
7.	Assessing applicable compliance requirements and planning ISO 27001 implementation.
Detailed costs, milestones, resource allocations, and ownership should be documented in the programme implementation plan.
12. Review and Approval
This charter should be reviewed at least annually and whenever significant organisational, regulatory, operational, or security changes require an update.
The CISO, once appointed, should coordinate reviews in consultation with relevant stakeholders. Until then, the CEO or a designated interim owner should coordinate the review.
Changes to the charter must be approved by the Board of Directors or the authority formally delegated by the board.

13. Conclusion
This Governance Charter establishes a proposed foundation for formalising VaultEdge's governance arrangements. It defines the responsibilities, decision-making principles, reporting expectations, and risk management requirements needed to support improved security oversight and compliance management.
Once approved, the charter should guide the development of the governance structure, RACI matrix, risk appetite statement, reporting arrangements, and performance indicators.
